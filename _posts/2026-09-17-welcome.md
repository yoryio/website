## What this blog is

I track how attacks begin — attack surface, adversary infrastructure, initial access,
and the OPSEC failures that let defenders and researchers pull an operation apart.
This is where I'll publish the write-ups: infrastructure-tracking investigations,
technical analysis of the N-days used for initial access, and notes from lab research
on phishing and delivery tradecraft.

## The focus

Two things shape most of what I'll write here:

- **Latin America.** The region is under-reported relative to its threat activity.
  Peru in particular has very little dedicated public research, which makes it a place
  where careful, well-sourced tracking work has room to matter.
- **Adversary infrastructure.** Domains, certificates, hosting patterns, and the
  fingerprints (JARM/JA4, favicon hashes, reused TLS certs) that cluster an actor's
  estate together. Every write-up will separate what's a *lead* from what's a
  *conclusion*, and keep link-confidence distinct from attribution-confidence.

## What's next

The first real investigation is an infrastructure-tracking study of **Blind Eagle
(APT-C-36)**, a threat actor active across Latin America. That post is in progress.

Everything here is for defensive research, detection, and authorized testing only.
